When Faster Is Not Qualified: Audited CPU, GPU and NPU Experiments on a Raspberry Pi 5.

Tyler Sevde — project owner and operator
Research and report drafting: ChatGPT Codex, identified by the project owner as
“ChatGPT Codex Astra Ultra.”
AI-assisted technical report; not externally peer reviewed.
Report date: 6 October 2026. Measurements discussed: 1–6 October 2026.

ABSTRACT

This project investigated whether measured software improvements on a Raspberry
Pi 5 could justify useful, correct changes to CPU, GPU and neural-accelerator
workflows. It began with a supplied synthetic attention demonstration and grew
into bounded experiments with frozen protocols, numerical oracles, fresh
fixtures, duplicate controls, cumulative time limits and independent offline
reconstruction. Earlier confirmation established a 2.85782-fold improvement of
one GPU implementation over another, while retaining a large disadvantage to
CPU execution. The final CPU comparison measured speedups of 1.21223 for single
requests and 1.19045 for sixteen-call batches, but unstable single-request
baseline duplicates prevented policy qualification. A twelve-shape GPU study
found no CPU crossover. A revised full-table language-model policy completed
96 of 96 planned answers validly but answered only 64 strictly correctly,
failing its original quality gate. Dependent cache and combined-system stages
therefore remained unrun. The completion allocation consumed 17,399.547867786954
of 72,000 seconds, including an inherited failed startup. No new preset was
exported and no application default changed. The contribution is a traceable
experimental record that distinguishes observed speed, reliable measurement,
task correctness and qualification. It does not demonstrate general language-
model acceleration, energy savings or recursive self-improvement.

1. ORIGIN, ATTRIBUTION AND QUESTIONS

The original archive, reference/algorithm_efficiency_experiment.zip, contains
three files: a Python demonstration, recorded results and an explanatory
readme. Tyler Sevde supplied it and attributes its creation to “ChatGPT Astra
Max” used through an iPadOS interface. That attribution describes the owner's
reported interaction. The results inside the archive identify an x86_64 Linux
execution environment, Python 3.12.14 and NumPy 2.3.5; they do not establish
that the benchmark computation ran on the iPad. The ZIP and its extracted files
are preserved byte-for-byte, with identities listed in Section 11.

The owner attributes subsequent experimental planning, implementation,
execution and fulfillment to ChatGPT Codex, using the label “ChatGPT Codex
Astra Ultra.” Those service/model labels are owner-supplied attribution, not
internal model identities established by experiment manifests. The user supplied
the hardware, access and authorization; the software agents prepared and ran
the authorized procedures. AI-assisted drafting and automated audit are not
external peer review or independent laboratory replication.

The starting demonstration compares established ways to evaluate a single
causal attention layer with fixed random inputs and weights. Attention relates
queries to keys and combines values, as developed in the Transformer literature
[R1]. Caching retains earlier keys and values so they need not be recomputed
for each new position; it exchanges persistent storage for less repeated work
[R2]. Neither operation is a newly discovered algorithm here.

The archive's recorded cached-versus-uncached median speedups were 2.459, 3.390
and 6.382 at sequence lengths 128, 256 and 512. These are supplied demonstration
results from the recorded x86_64 environment, not the final Pi policy results.
The demonstration used float64, one requested numerical-library thread, seven
timed repetitions, and a primary uncached baseline that computes the newest
query while recomputing earlier keys and values. Its full-prefix reference is
a separate, less efficient implementation. Baseline choice matters when
interpreting the size of a gain.

The later roadmap asked five practical questions: could a CPU operating policy
qualify against defaults; could stream64 GPU execution become useful against
CPU over a bounded matrix; could the installed NPU model meet the full-table
quality requirement; would qualified context reuse help; and would a qualified
combined route help? The last two questions were conditional. Completion meant
an honest disposition for each question, including “not run,” rather than
requiring a positive finding from every device. [E0, E1]

The machine was recorded as Raspberry Pi 5 Model B Rev 1.1, with four CPU cores

The numerical backend used C++17 and OpenMP, compiled by GCC 14.2.0 with -O3,
-mcpu=cortex-a76 and -ffp-contract=off. The GPU path used Vulkan compute shaders
and recorded hardware identity “V3D 7.1.10.2; vendor=5348; driver=109060098.”
Frozen build receipts retain compiler flags, shader compiler versions, source
digests, SPIR-V and binary hashes. These measured identities are more precise
reproduction requirements than simply requesting “a Raspberry Pi.” [E2, E3]

The attached accelerator was an AI HAT+ 2. Manufacturer documentation identifies
that board's accelerator as Hailo-10H and describes its separate onboard memory
[R3]; advertised operation rates are not measurements made by this project.
The final NPU studies used HailoRT 5.1.1 and the installed
Llama-3_2-3B-Instruct.hef, 3,370,416,230 bytes. Earlier work used Qwen2.5-1.5B;
those results must remain distinct. Meta's model card supplies background for
the Llama family [R4], but the HEF hash identifies the actual deployed artifact.
This report does not assume that the original model card's context capacity
or benchmark results describe the compiled Hailo package.

The HailoRT API exposes tokenization, context accounting, clear/save/load
operations and configurable stop tokens [R5]. Their existence does not prove
that a particular cache policy is useful or correct. Final qualification
rebuilt the context every turn; retained-context and checkpoint alternatives
were conditional future measurements, not completed final-stage results.

3. DESIGN AND STATISTICAL INTERPRETATION

Source, protocol, model, workload and applicable native identities were frozen
before measurement. Development and confirmation used separate fixture
namespaces. Pilots were charged to their stage but excluded from primary
statistics. Pilot feasibility retained a 20% margin and 120 seconds for cleanup.
An infeasible full matrix was to stop rather than shrink after observing data.
Failed startup attempts remained separate evidence with their time charged.

For native attention, every output was compared with an FP64 oracle evaluated
on the same FP32 inputs. The acceptance bound was absolute tolerance 1e-5 plus
relative tolerance 1e-4 times the reference magnitude. This differs from the
original float64 demonstration's 1e-10 tolerances. Semantic GPU controls and
validation checks supplemented numerical comparison. A fast incorrect output
could not qualify.

The performance gate required at least 1.10-fold aggregate speedup, a paired
95% bootstrap lower bound above one, no tested shape more than 5% slower, and
stable duplicate controls. Stability required the whole duplicate-ratio 95%
interval to lie within 0.95–1.05, both overall and per shape. Merely overlapping
one was insufficient. This protects against declaring a policy useful while
nominally identical controls disagree beyond the permitted uncertainty.

The bootstrap is a resampling approach to estimating statistical uncertainty
[R6]. Here, the resampling unit was the whole paired fixture block, preserving
the association between implementations on the same input. Repetitions and
duplicates were averaged within shape before equal shape weighting. Ten
thousand resamples were used, with frozen seeds. Treating sixteen calls within
one batch, or four turns within one dialogue, as independent experiments would
inflate the apparent sample size. They were not treated that way.

A ratio above one means the numerator implementation took longer: for a
baseline/candidate ratio, 1.20 represents a candidate taking approximately
83.3% of baseline time. It is not a 20% reduction in elapsed time. Bootstrap
intervals describe uncertainty under this sampling design. They do not remove
systematic measurement bias or establish performance on another machine.

Native timing ran from submission through receipt of a validated result,
including transport and applicable transfers and synchronization; initialization
was reported separately. Frozen route summaries assembled times from measured
shape-specific calls. They did not separately time a production dispatcher.
Workers also recorded process ownership, affinity, environment and telemetry.
Temporary governor settings were restored. Temperature, memory, throttling and
deadline checks protected execution, but do not turn this into a power study.

4. CPU: A GAIN THAT DID NOT QUALIFY

Earlier CPU confirmation established that policy 5 was repeatable on its tested
workload. It did not compare that policy with application defaults. The final
experiment supplied that missing comparison: ondemand governor with default
OpenMP environment versus performance governor, bound threads and passive
waiting. Native code and per-shape thread routing were identical between arms.
All present OMP_/GOMP_ overrides were cleared before applying each declared
configuration; numerical-library thread environment values remained fixed.

The matrix comprised N=128, 512 and 1024; D=64; and tensor B=1 or 4. It used
256 fresh paired main blocks, three repetitions and duplicate single-request
and sixteen-call batch arms. Configuration order alternated; arm order covered
all 24 permutations over eight blocks. The audit counted 36,864 main measurement
events plus 144 excluded pilot events. A batch comprised sixteen sequential
native calls, not a new device-side batching algorithm. [E2]

Table 1. Final CPU results; batch milliseconds cover all sixteen calls.

Endpoint     Baseline ms  Candidate ms  Speedup   Paired 95% interval   Stable?
Single          24.4197       20.1444    1.212229  [1.187793,1.237565]   No
16-call batch  367.9677      309.0988    1.190453  [1.183802,1.197340]   Yes

Numerical correctness passed. Maximum candidate/baseline per-shape slowdown
was 1.012470 for singles and 1.005945 for batches, within the 1.05 guard.
However, baseline single-request duplicate intervals were approximately
[0.991768,1.174850] at N512/B4 and [0.897799,0.993308] at N1024/B4. Neither
interval was contained within the required equivalence range.

The batch endpoint passed its gates, while the single endpoint did not. Because
the frozen decision required both, the policy was rejected for qualification.
This is evidence of an aggregate measured gain with insufficient control
stability for the promised combined claim, not proof that the candidate is
universally slower. Selecting a batch-only preset after seeing the failure
would change the research question after measurement. No such selection or
preset export occurred, and application defaults remained unchanged.

Two related startup failures are also retained: the inherited 5 October attempt
and a new 6 October authentication timeout. They produced no pilot or matrix
measurements. Their dispositions are infrastructure failures, not adverse
performance observations. Their elapsed allowances still count. [E1]

5. GPU: IMPLEMENTATION IMPROVEMENT WITHOUT CPU CROSSOVER

On 4 October, an earlier 64-block confirmation found stream64 2.857821 times
faster than its old GPU baseline, with paired 95% interval
[2.850774,2.863870]. That confirmation passed its scoped correctness and
stability requirements. Mean stream64 request time nevertheless remained
22.227635 times CPU time over the six tested shapes. It established a GPU
implementation improvement, not a reason to replace CPU execution. [E5]

The final development study broadened the workload to twelve shapes:
N=128/512/1024, D=64/128 and tensor B=1/16. Its four arms were CPU, duplicate
CPU, old GPU and stream64. It used eight main blocks, three repetitions and
one excluded pilot block, yielding 1,152 main and 144 pilot measurements.
The fixed CPU reference used native1 for B1 and native4 for B16; it was not a
search for globally optimal CPU thread counts. Tensor B represents independent
sequences within one native call, not repeated submission of individual jobs.

Development was to select GPU only for shapes achieving at least 1.10-fold
CPU-relative speedup with lower confidence bound above one, then require the
aggregate routed policy to pass all gates. Confirmation would use fresh
fixtures with routing frozen. The matrix and budget forecast were set before
measurement; larger extrapolated workloads were excluded in the protocol
because the complete comparison was not expected to fit the stage. [E3]

Table 2. Different GPU claims have different denominators and evidence stages.

Study                   Old-GPU / stream64 speedup   Paired 95% interval
Earlier confirmation               2.857821           [2.850774,2.863870]
Final development                  3.447532           [3.440004,3.454484]

Final CPU/stream64 speedup across twelve shapes ranged from 0.047272 to
0.119012: stream64 took roughly 8.4–21.2 times CPU time. No shape crossed the
CPU threshold. Numerical checks passed, but per-shape CPU duplicate equivalence
also failed. Thus no route qualified and no fresh confirmation was opened.
The 3.447532 development result must not be presented as another independently
confirmed result or combined statistically with the older matrix.

All proposed shape routes remained CPU, producing a composed route ratio of
exactly 1.0. That number reuses the measured CPU baseline for CPU-selected
shapes. It is not a timed dynamic-dispatch result with zero overhead. The study
supports a bounded negative crossover finding, alongside a control-stability
limitation; it does not show that every GPU workload on this machine is useless.

6. NPU: DIAGNOSIS DID NOT REPLACE THE ORIGINAL TASK

The NPU task required four-turn color lookup from a complete table, retaining
actual prior user prompts and raw assistant answers. Correctness used the
unchanged strict whole-answer rubric. Mentioning the expected color among
extra answers or prose did not silently become success. A completed response
and a correct response were distinct outcomes.

A fresh diagnostic crossed complete-table versus CPU-selected single-fact
input with actual-history versus independent questions. Every arm had 96
planned answers: eight main fixture blocks, three size groups and four turns.
Selected-fact input removes much of the retrieval burden; independent questions
remove accumulated conversation. Neither intervention satisfies the original
full-table, actual-history qualification task. All diagnostic arms used the
frozen punctuation-stop condition. [E4]

Table 3. Diagnostic results; every arm retains denominator 96.

Input / history                Observed  Valid  Strict correct  Quarantined
Full table / actual history          83     70        44              13
Full table / independent             96     94        80               0
Selected fact / actual history       94     89        55               2
Selected fact / independent          96     96        94               0

The pattern motivated one separately frozen revision instructing exact item
lookup, excluding previous assistant answers as factual authority, and requiring
consistent formatting on repeated questions. It did not justify treating the
94/96 simpler-arm score as full-table qualification. The candidate was tested
on new development fixtures, against an unchanged native-stop full-table
control. Greedy generation used do_sample=false, seed 12345 and a maximum of
32 generated tokens; the exact parameter values are retained in protocol.json.

Table 4. Revised full-table development; planned denominator remains 96.

Arm          Observed  Valid  Strict correct  Correct by size     Quarantined
Native stop      46      24       3           0/32, 3/32, 0/32         50
Candidate        96      96      64          22/32,19/32,23/32          0

Development required 96/96 valid, at least 92/96 strictly correct, at least
29/32 at every size and zero contract failures. The candidate passed validity
and contracts but failed both overall and per-size correctness. The paired
development accuracy gain over its matched native control was 0.635417, with
95% interval [0.489583,0.781250]. A positive improvement from a weak control
does not satisfy an absolute quality requirement. No confirmation ran. [E6]

The current matched control scored 3/96, not the diagnostic 44/96. Those scores
come from different fresh datasets and different frozen stop conditions; the
diagnostic used punctuation stopping, while the qualification control preserved
native stops. Substituting the earlier score would destroy the matched
comparison. The revised candidate retained its declared punctuation-stop policy.
This is a policy comparison, not an isolated estimate of prompt wording alone.

Truncated or invalid answers remained failures. When an invalid history could
not safely continue, later turns were quarantined, not silently removed.
For the native qualification control, 46 observed answers plus 50 quarantined
turns still total 96 planned answers. No CPU-corrected answer counted as raw
model success. The audit reconstructed these denominators from saved rows.

Table sizing also needs care. The nominal groups describe the largest original-
control first prompt at or below a target, not equal actual token counts for
every arm. Both qualification arms received identical complete tables. The
longer candidate instruction increased prompt size without changing the facts.

Table 5. Main-request token ranges; unobserved turns have no invented counts.

Control-sized group     128          512          1024
Complete-table facts     14           91           193
Native first prompt     126          511          1021
Candidate first prompt  222          607          1117
Native observed range   126–197      511–586      1021–1056
Candidate range         222–282      607–659      1117–1168

Had development passed, fresh confirmation would have required 192/192 valid,
at least 183/192 strictly correct, at least 58/64 per size, zero contracts,
and a minimum five-percentage-point gain over the native control with paired
95% lower gain bound above zero. None of those confirmation outcomes was
measured. The failed development gate closed policy-aware cache and combined
testing. Earlier cache or combined observations cannot reopen those gates.

8. REPRODUCIBILITY, AUDIT AND PRESERVATION

Each completed stage preserves protocol, source snapshot, fixtures, schedule,
raw events, outputs where applicable, telemetry, summaries, plots, validation
and a checksum inventory. Auditors independently reconstruct scheduled coverage,
numerical checks or answer scores, statistical decisions, budget lineage and
restoration. The frozen implementation passed 589 regression tests; separate
checkpoint tooling passed 47 disposable checks. Those tests support software
behavior, not scientific claims about hardware speed or model accuracy.

The word “independent” refers to reconstruction from raw evidence rather than
trusting a saved summary. The auditors were part of this AI-assisted project,
not outside peer reviewers. Hashes detect changes relative to a trusted pin;
an attacker could otherwise replace files and generate new hashes. Pinned
historical inventories and deliberately altered/resealed-evidence tests provide
stronger checks, but no offline program proves that a past physical execution
occurred exactly as reported.

Saved NPU token IDs and counts were checked for consistency without replaying
the hardware tokenizer. Recorded affinity, process and clock observations were
checked without rerunning the machine. Another investigator should distinguish
this evidence reconstruction from a fresh replication, which requires compatible
hardware, the pinned model/runtime, new output directories and a new approved
budget. A model name alone does not identify identical compiled bytes.

The 6 October checkpoint underwent an actual independent USB-to-SSD restoration
test, verified at 17:22:02.816935 UTC and certified at 17:25:42.471260 UTC. Its
receipt records 19,924 inventory entries and 74 canonical seals. The receipt
is external to the immutable closure, avoiding a circular requirement to seal
proof of an archive before that archive can be restored. That checkpoint
predates this publication document; its success does not certify a later
publication package or a future HDD/GitHub copy. [E7]

9. LIMITATIONS AND INTERPRETATION

These are measurements on one system, within bounded shapes and tasks. Native
attention uses synthetic weights and inputs, not an entire deployed LLM.
The NPU experiment measures a particular compiled model and strict lookup task.
A disappointing score does not establish general model incapability, just as
a speedup over an inefficient implementation does not establish superiority
to a stronger baseline. Direct CPU lookup already solves the color task; this
study does not establish an application need for language-model inference.

Eight development blocks provide less sampling coverage than the larger CPU
and earlier GPU confirmations. Unstable duplicate controls constrain performance
conclusions. Equal shape weighting is a declared experimental choice, not a
measurement of a user's real workload distribution. Shape-specific routing is
composed from complete calls; dynamic-dispatch latency remains unmeasured.
No trained model was partitioned across CPU, GPU and NPU.

Energy and accelerator cache bytes were not measured. Host memory, temperature,
clock and throttling records are not substitutes for energy measurements.
Likewise, a one-time component improvement cannot establish repeated exponential
gains. There was no demonstrated autonomous recursive improvement process,
singularity, universal fivefold-per-iteration law or change in model intelligence.

The useful outcome is disciplined separation of claims. A faster component can
remain the wrong route; a repeatable configuration can lack a qualified gain
against defaults; a valid answer can be wrong; and a complete investigation can
end with conditional stages unrun. Here those distinctions preserved the
original gates, left defaults unchanged and produced evidence that another
reader can inspect without accepting the narrative on trust.
