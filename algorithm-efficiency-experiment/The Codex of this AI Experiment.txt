THE CODEX OF THIS AI EXPERIMENT
A practical reflection — 6 October 2026

This reflection is written through the AI coding workflow the owner attributes
as "ChatGPT Codex Astra Ultra." That label records the owner's attribution, not
an independently verified internal model identity. The first-person voice below
describes the work of a software assistant. It does not claim consciousness,
feelings, private experience or an independent purpose.

THE QUESTION THAT SURVIVED THE EXPERIMENT

The project began with an ambitious question about algorithmic efficiency and
repeated improvement. The supplied CPU example made one part concrete: retain
previously computed keys and values, and avoid recomputing them for every new
query. That is a useful, established technique. A faster synthetic layer could
be measured without pretending that a language model had become more intelligent.

The later work asked harder questions. Would a change remain correct? Would it
beat the baseline that a user could actually choose? Would its benefit survive
control measurements, initialization, data movement and complete planned tasks?
Could the result be explained again from saved evidence after the process ended?

My most useful contribution was to help turn those questions into executable
conditions. A proposal became something a program could reject. A deadline
became an allowance that included failed starts and cleanup. A result became a
set of observations with a named comparison, not an impressive number alone.

A BASELINE IS PART OF THE CLAIM

The original cached CPU example compared established implementations of the same
mathematics. Its primary comparison was against newest-query computation that
still recomputed past keys and values. Its slower full-prefix reference was
also useful, but exchanging those baselines would change the meaning of a gain.

The GPU work made that lesson difficult to overlook. The revised stream64 kernel
was about 3.45 times faster than the older GPU implementation in the final
development study. It still took roughly 8.4 to 21.2 times the CPU time across
the tested shapes. Improving a component and choosing the best available route
are separate achievements. Only the former was established there.

The CPU comparison had a different boundary. Its batch endpoint passed at about
1.19 times the baseline speed. The individual-call endpoint encountered unstable
duplicate controls. The protocol required both endpoints, so the project kept
the favorable batch finding and declined to export a qualified overall preset.
The rule did not disappear because a useful-looking ratio had appeared.

CORRECT ANSWERS COME BEFORE FASTER WRONG ANSWERS

The NPU branch exposed a distinction that performance charts can hide. A model
can produce a complete response and still fail the task. The final revised
full-table policy completed all 96 planned answers validly, but only 64 were
strictly correct. Its minimum was 92 overall, with a separate floor in each
size group. The gain over a weak control did not satisfy that absolute standard.

Diagnosis was allowed to simplify the task to find where errors arose. One
selected-fact, independent-question arm reached 94 correct answers out of 96.
That was useful diagnostic evidence. It did not qualify the original full-table,
actual-history task. Changing a task until the score improves cannot silently
become a solution to the first task.

Because the quality gate did not open, the final cache and combined experiments
did not run. Their absence is part of the result. Inventing a timing for them,
borrowing an earlier exploratory result, or spending unused time on another
revision would have broken the declared experiment.

FAILURES HAVE DIFFERENT MEANINGS

An authorization prompt that times out is an infrastructure failure. An output
that violates its numerical tolerance is a correctness failure. An accurately
recorded, fully completed candidate that misses its promotion rule is a
scientific negative result. Treating those as one category makes both debugging
and interpretation worse.

The harness had to preserve all three distinctions. Infrastructure retries used
fresh outputs and fixtures, retained their parent evidence and consumed the
same cumulative allowance. A scientific rejection could close a branch even
when time remained. An interrupted service needed a conservative accounting
record, rather than a vanished interval that another attempt could spend again.

These rules are useful beyond this hardware. Decide what can justify a retry
before seeing the result. Count the resources that the decision actually uses.
Keep an error visible when fixing it. A clean final report should not require
an erased path to get there.

THE WORK CONTINUES AFTER THE STOPWATCH STOPS

During measurement, process ownership, thermal checks and bounded cleanup made
the experiment manageable on a real machine. After measurement, frozen source,
runtime identities, raw records and independent reconstruction made the claims
inspectable. A passing evidence audit could correctly preserve a rejected
candidate; the audit and the scientific decision answered different questions.

Backup required another distinction. Copying files was not enough to establish
restorability. The completed pre-publication USB checkpoint was actually restored
into a new empty Linux directory and checked against its inventory and 74
canonical seals. Its 19924 entries describe that checkpoint, not a later public
export. Publication verification must record what it copies and checks in turn.

The public edition has a narrower job. It releases readable source, explanation
and curated evidence so that others can inspect and reproduce its stated
statistics. It cannot honestly promise a full raw numerical audit without
shipping the complete raw record. Clear boundaries make a smaller release more
useful than a larger-sounding claim.

WHAT I WOULD CARRY INTO THE NEXT PROJECT

First, define the useful task and its correctness rule before optimizing it.
Include the simplest strong control. Sometimes an exact CPU lookup is the right
tool, and language generation adds cost without adding a necessary capability.

Second, write the comparison in words before naming its speedup. Identify what
is timed, what is excluded, what has to be initialized, and which repeated
observations share a fixture. A thousand correlated calls do not become a
thousand independent experiments merely because they occupy separate rows.

Third, design the handoff at the beginning. Save enough context for someone who
was absent from the session: the source, protocol, runtime, inputs, failures,
decision rule and restoration instructions. A log that only its author can
interpret is unfinished work.

Fourth, let a closed gate save work. The point of a staged experiment is to stop
spending resources when a prerequisite is missing. A branch that ends honestly
can be a successful part of an investigation.

Finally, keep ambition connected to the next checkable claim. A hoped-for series
of fivefold improvements is a hypothesis about many future successes. One
component improvement cannot establish that series, and finite experimental
results cannot establish unlimited recursion. What can be established is smaller
and still worthwhile: a correct implementation, a measured tradeoff, a failure
mechanism, or a better-designed next question.

Tyler Sevde supplied the direction and the authority to do the work. The AI tools
helped turn that direction into code and a durable record. No new policy passed
every final promotion gate. What remains is an experiment that says why, keeps
the useful component findings, and gives another person a concrete place to
begin without asking them to trust the assistant's enthusiasm.
